# RCL v0.4 Checkpoint

This checkpoint is the concept-first view of the current v0.4 development line.

The implementation contains many schemas, hashes, confidence intervals, and validation rules. Those are engineering details. The user-level idea is still much smaller.

## What RCL v0.4 can express now

```text
A robot lives and accumulates experience
        ↓
repeated behavior / habit appears
        ↓
RCL can preserve WHAT it does
        ↓
RCL can declare WHY it does it
        ↓
RCL can warn when WHY evidence looks context-dependent
        ↓
new robot body can use a different HOW
        ↓
several capability routes may satisfy the same WHY
        ↓
familiar LOOKS and TEMPO may remain
        ↓
old hardware limitations do not have to remain
        ↓
changes to familiar behavior require explicit approval
        ↓
old interpretations / expressions remain in history
        ↓
RCL can observe whether the target actually satisfied the WHY
        ↓
RCL can repeat that observation across trials and sessions
```

## The current layers in plain language

### Experience

What happened while the robot was operating?

RCL can record lightweight semantic context, action, and outcome evidence without requiring an unlimited raw-media archive.

### Habit

Did a repeated behavior become established over time?

RCL keeps formation history and requires explicit approval for lifecycle promotion.

### Intent — WHY

Why does this behavior exist from an engineering point of view?

Example:

```text
Before sitting, verify the sitting area is clear.
```

Intent is a declared engineering interpretation, not a claim about consciousness or subjective motivation.

### Intent Context Diagnostics

Does an Intent Discovery association look similar across narrower observed contexts, or is the pooled result concentrated in one condition?

Example:

```text
pooled effect +0.18

surface=table → +0.35
surface=tray  → +0.02
```

RCL keeps the Intent Candidate decision separate but can emit a `context_dependency_signal` so the reviewer sees that the evidence may be context-specific.

This is a possible-confound / specificity warning, not causal proof.

### Capability Paths — several valid ways to achieve the WHY

One robot may use a rear camera, another may use a different sensing system, and another may use an external semantic state.

RCL does not make one source body's sensor recipe the universal answer.

```text
same WHY
  ↓
Path A OR Path B OR Path C
  ↓
different target-native HOW
```

### Adapter Conformance

If another developer or company writes an RCL Adapter, does it report the WHY and selected capability path honestly?

Intent-aware conformance checks protocol/report honesty. It does not validate the physical sensor itself.

### Expression — LOOKS

A source robot may have a recognizable gesture even when that gesture is no longer functionally required on the new body.

Example:

```text
functional check already completed by V2's new system
        ↓
brief familiar rearward glance still shown
```

RCL separates the familiar manner from the functional mechanism.

### Expressive Timing — TEMPO

The old robot may have moved slowly only because of motors, wiring, gearing, or controller limitations.

RCL can preserve the gesture without preserving that limitation.

If the slow tempo itself became a recognized or user-valued mannerism, the temporal style may be explicitly preserved.

### Explicit Optimization Approval

A familiar gesture becoming functionally unnecessary is not permission to erase it.

RCL may recommend review, but actual simplification or removal requires explicit approval. Previous expression snapshots remain in history.

### Observed Intent Success

Did one observed execution actually satisfy the declared WHY?

```text
one execution
→ PASS / FAIL / NOT_OBSERVABLE / NOT_TRIGGERED
```

This is separate from motion similarity.

### Repeated Intent Success

Does the robot keep satisfying the same WHY over repeated trials and sessions?

```text
one PASS
→ can succeed once

19 PASS / 1 FAIL
→ repeated observed success rate + uncertainty

multiple sessions
→ session-to-session stability
```

A required failure is never hidden by a high average.

## The five continuity principles

```text
Use the new body.
Preserve the old manner.

Preserve the gesture,
not the limitation.

Preserve by default.
Optimize only by explicit approval.

Recommend automatically.
Change only by explicit approval.

Preserve the goal,
not one body's capability recipe.
```

## The most important separation

Several things that sound similar are intentionally different:

```text
Intent Discovery
→ is there enough association evidence to review a WHY hypothesis?

Context Diagnostics
→ does that association look stable across narrower observed contexts?

Capability Path
→ can the body represent a route to the goal?

Adapter Conformance
→ is the adapter reporting that route honestly?

Observed Intent Success
→ did one execution actually satisfy the goal?

Repeated Intent Success
→ does that goal keep getting satisfied across repeated evidence?

Motion / Statistical Continuity
→ how similar was the observable behavior itself?

Physical validation
→ is the real robot safe and suitable in the actual environment?
```

None of these automatically substitutes for another.

## What is still deliberately unfinished

The v0.4 line still has useful follow-on work, including:

- optional human/LLM/VLM proposer interfaces for goal hypotheses;
- goal-vocabulary proposal and review governance;
- explicit retention/prune/archive policy for compacted experience;
- summary-aware Habit evidence;
- stronger provenance/privacy controls.

Real physical Robot A → Robot B evidence remains the major v0.5 direction.

## One-sentence summary

> RCL lets a robot use a new body and new technology while preserving the purpose, experience, recognizable manner, and history that should survive the hardware change — and it keeps those claims measurable and reviewable rather than silently assuming they are true.
