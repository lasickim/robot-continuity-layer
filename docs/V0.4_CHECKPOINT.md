# RCL v0.4 Checkpoint

This checkpoint is the concept-first view of the current v0.4 development line.

The implementation contains many schemas, hashes, confidence intervals, and validation rules. Those are engineering details. The user-level idea is still much smaller.

## What RCL v0.4 can express now

```text
A robot lives and accumulates experience
        ↓
experience can be compacted without deleting its source
        ↓
verified retention policy can decide what stays active / may be archived / may later be pruned
        ↓
provenance/privacy governance can carry lineage and sharing restrictions across derived artifacts
        ↓
raw or aggregate evidence can support Habit review without inventing pseudo-episodes
        ↓
repeated behavior / habit appears
        ↓
RCL can preserve WHAT it does
        ↓
human / rule / LLM / VLM may propose a WHY hypothesis
        ↓
observed evidence is evaluated separately
        ↓
RCL can declare WHY only through the existing review/approval path
        ↓
RCL can warn when WHY evidence looks context-dependent
        ↓
experimental WHY names can be proposed for shared vocabulary review
        ↓
new robot body can use a different HOW
        ↓
several capability routes may satisfy the same WHY
        ↓
familiar LOOKS and TEMPO may remain
        ↓
old hardware limitations do not have to remain
        ↓
changes to familiar behavior require explicit approval
        ↓
old interpretations / expressions remain in history
        ↓
RCL can observe whether the target actually satisfied the WHY
        ↓
RCL can repeat that observation across trials and sessions
```

## The current layers in plain language

### Experience

What happened while the robot was operating?

RCL can record lightweight semantic context, action, and outcome evidence without requiring an unlimited raw-media archive.

Compaction can turn repeated episodes into long-lived aggregate evidence while preserving source provenance and action-present/action-absent statistics.

### Experience Retention

How do we stop raw experience from growing forever without silently forgetting history?

RCL separates compaction from lifecycle decisions:

```text
raw episodes
   ↓
compaction
   ↓
summary
   ↓
verify summary against the exact current source
   ↓
retention policy
   ↓
RETAIN | ARCHIVE_CANDIDATE | PRUNE_CANDIDATE
```

The important rule is:

> **Compaction is not deletion consent.**

`prune_candidate` does not mean deleted. It means only that a later explicit prune workflow may consider that episode.

The conservative reference policy protects recent evidence, retained summary exemplars, external evidence links, sparse groups, and a deterministic per-group remainder.

Archive records are also deliberately modest: they record a deployment assertion that an external copy exists, but RCL does not claim to have inspected remote storage bytes.

### Provenance / Privacy Governance

Where did a continuity artifact come from, and does knowing that provenance mean it may be shared?

No. v0.4 makes that separation explicit.

> **Provenance is not permission.**

A companion provenance/privacy record can bind a JSON artifact to:

```text
artifact SHA-256
origin / actor
parent record lineage
transformation metadata
privacy classification
sharing scope
external evidence-reference policy
```

Derived artifacts are monotonic by default:

```text
private parent
   ↓ compaction / derivation
private-or-more-restrictive child
```

They cannot silently become less private, broaden their sharing scope, or expand external evidence-reference permissions.

The second principle is:

> **Aggregation does not automatically declassify data.**

Operation review is deterministic and non-mutating:

```text
local_use
share_approved
share_public
archive
prune_review
      ↓
ALLOWED | BLOCKED
```

RCL does not inspect content to decide whether something is legally personal information, and it does not execute sharing, archival, evidence copying, or pruning.

### Habit

Did a repeated behavior become established over time?

RCL keeps formation history and requires explicit approval for lifecycle promotion.

### Summary-aware Habit Evidence

Can old compacted experience still support Habit review after raw evidence has moved through its retention lifecycle?

Yes, but RCL keeps the provenance explicit.

```text
RAW Experience
→ direct_source

Compacted Summary
→ aggregate
→ summary_declared

Compacted Summary + original source available
→ aggregate
→ raw_verified
```

Raw and aggregate reports expose comparable counts, repeat rate, observation span, and semantic-group coverage, but aggregate evidence never pretends to be raw experience.

The rule is:

> **Aggregate evidence may support Habit review, but it must never pretend to be raw experience.**

A `sufficient` Habit Evidence Report means the repeated semantic action pattern is substantial enough to support review under the selected policy. It does not itself make a behavior `stable` or `legacy`.

Existing Habit Promotion remains unchanged unless a deployment explicitly supplies a Habit Evidence Report as an additional formation-evidence gate. No pseudo-episodes or synthetic `habit.events` are created.

### Intent Hypothesis Proposers

Who is allowed to suggest what the WHY might be?

In v0.4, a human, rule engine, LLM, VLM, external model, or other proposer can all submit the same neutral proposal envelope.

```text
human / rules / LLM / VLM
        ↓
WHY hypothesis proposal
        ↓
actual evidence stays separate
        ↓
Discovery / Context Diagnostics / Approval
```

A proposer never receives approval authority just because it is a model or because it reports a high confidence value.

```text
model self-confidence = audit metadata
RCL confidence = evidence evaluation
```

RCL stores a concise reviewable rationale, not hidden chain-of-thought.

The principle is:

> **Proposers may suggest the WHY. Evidence and explicit approval decide whether that WHY becomes continuity metadata.**

### Intent — WHY

Why does this behavior exist from an engineering point of view?

Example:

```text
Before sitting, verify the sitting area is clear.
```

Intent is a declared engineering interpretation, not a claim about consciousness or subjective motivation.

### Intent Context Diagnostics

Does an Intent Discovery association look similar across narrower observed contexts, or is the pooled result concentrated in one condition?

Example:

```text
pooled effect +0.18

surface=table → +0.35
surface=tray  → +0.02
```

RCL keeps the Intent Candidate decision separate but can emit a `context_dependency_signal` so the reviewer sees that the evidence may be context-specific.

This is a possible-confound / specificity warning, not causal proof.

### Goal Vocabulary Governance

Projects can freely experiment with private/vendor goals under:

```text
x.<owner>.<semantic_path>
```

When someone wants one of those ideas to become a shared RCL standard goal, governance adds a review layer:

```text
experimental WHY
        ↓
shared vocabulary proposal
        ↓
duplicate / semantic overlap / body-specificity checks
        ↓
human decision
        ↓
explicit repository/spec vocabulary change
```

The governance tool never silently edits the bundled standard vocabulary.

The principle is:

> **Experiment freely; standardize deliberately.**

### Capability Paths — several valid ways to achieve the WHY

One robot may use a rear camera, another may use a different sensing system, and another may use an external semantic state.

RCL does not make one source body's sensor recipe the universal answer.

```text
same WHY
  ↓
Path A OR Path B OR Path C
  ↓
different target-native HOW
```

### Adapter Conformance

If another developer or company writes an RCL Adapter, does it report the WHY and selected capability path honestly?

Intent-aware conformance checks protocol/report honesty. It does not validate the physical sensor itself.

### Expression — LOOKS

A source robot may have a recognizable gesture even when that gesture is no longer functionally required on the new body.

Example:

```text
functional check already completed by V2's new system
        ↓
brief familiar rearward glance still shown
```

RCL separates the familiar manner from the functional mechanism.

### Expressive Timing — TEMPO

The old robot may have moved slowly only because of motors, wiring, gearing, or controller limitations.

RCL can preserve the gesture without preserving that limitation.

If the slow tempo itself became a recognized or user-valued mannerism, the temporal style may be explicitly preserved.

### Explicit Optimization Approval

A familiar gesture becoming functionally unnecessary is not permission to erase it.

RCL may recommend review, but actual simplification or removal requires explicit approval. Previous expression snapshots remain in history.

### Observed Intent Success

Did one observed execution actually satisfy the declared WHY?

```text
one execution
→ PASS / FAIL / NOT_OBSERVABLE / NOT_TRIGGERED
```

This is separate from motion similarity.

### Repeated Intent Success

Does the robot keep satisfying the same WHY over repeated trials and sessions?

```text
one PASS
→ can succeed once

19 PASS / 1 FAIL
→ repeated observed success rate + uncertainty

multiple sessions
→ session-to-session stability
```

A required failure is never hidden by a high average.

## The five continuity principles

```text
Use the new body.
Preserve the old manner.

Preserve the gesture,
not the limitation.

Preserve by default.
Optimize only by explicit approval.

Recommend automatically.
Change only by explicit approval.

Preserve the goal,
not one body's capability recipe.
```

## The most important separation

Several things that sound similar are intentionally different:

```text
Experience Compaction
→ can repeated raw evidence be represented as a non-destructive aggregate?

Experience Retention
→ what should stay active, what may be archived, and what may later be considered for pruning?

Provenance / Privacy Governance
→ where did this artifact come from, what restrictions did it inherit, and is the requested operation allowed?

Habit Evidence
→ is there enough repeated semantic action evidence to support Habit review, and is it RAW or AGGREGATE?

Habit Promotion
→ do history, confidence, reproducibility, and any explicitly supplied formation evidence justify a lifecycle review candidate?

Intent Proposer
→ who/what suggested a possible WHY?

Intent Discovery
→ is there enough association evidence to review that WHY hypothesis?

Context Diagnostics
→ does that association look stable across narrower observed contexts?

Intent Approval
→ may this reviewed WHY become current continuity metadata?

Goal Vocabulary Governance
→ should an experimental WHY name/definition be proposed as shared vocabulary?

Capability Path
→ can the body represent a route to the goal?

Adapter Conformance
→ is the adapter reporting that route honestly?

Observed Intent Success
→ did one execution actually satisfy the goal?

Repeated Intent Success
→ does that goal keep getting satisfied across repeated evidence?

Motion / Statistical Continuity
→ how similar was the observable behavior itself?

Physical validation
→ is the real robot safe and suitable in the actual environment?
```

None of these automatically substitutes for another.

## v0.4 completion boundary

With the provenance/privacy governance layer, the major planned v0.4 software/spec capability areas are represented in the reference implementation.

The major v0.4 capability surface is now frozen and the high-level documentation consolidation is complete. The remaining v0.4 work is a **release / checkpoint validation pass**, not automatic feature accumulation:

- run full supported-Python regression validation;
- fix only bug, safety, validation, schema-consistency, or documentation defects found by that pass;
- keep terminology and cross-links aligned with the frozen overview;
- preserve backward compatibility and the existing package-format boundary;
- finalize the v0.4 release/checkpoint record without adding new conceptual capability areas.

Real physical Robot A → Robot B evidence remains the major v0.5 direction.

## One-sentence summary

> RCL lets a robot use a new body and new technology while preserving the purpose, experience, recognizable manner, and history that should survive the hardware change — and it keeps those claims measurable, traceable, privacy-governed, and reviewable rather than silently assuming they are true.
